Agents with a human on the final click

Agents draft. People send.

Kinsman Consulting · 10 November 2026

We write and publish with the help of AI tools, under our editorial policy. Corrections: desk@kinsmanconsultings.com.

AI can now write the email, sort the inbox and fill in the weekly report. Most owners still don't trust it to work unsupervised. In Bluevine's 2026 survey of 942 US small-business owners, 78% "don't fully trust AI to handle low-level tasks without oversight."

We think that instinct is right. So we built around it rather than against it.

The expensive mistake is the one that leaves the building

A wrong draft costs a minute to fix. A wrong email to a client costs a good deal more. That makes the useful question not "can the agent write it?" but "who presses send?"

Our answer is the same every time: the agent does the legwork, and a person makes the final click.

What that looks like in practice

Kinsman Standing Orders is an operating routine that runs in your own Claude account. It gives you a morning brief, inbox sorting rules, a weekly scorecard, an approval queue, and hand-offs that carry a check-in date. Nothing goes out unless you've approved it. We never ask for or store your passwords.

Three habits of a good approval queue

  1. One line per item. Say what it is, who it goes to, why, and what you recommend. If approving takes longer than doing, the queue is badly built.
  2. One sitting, on a set schedule. Batch the approvals, daily or weekly, so you aren't pinged for each one.
  3. Every hand-off has a date. When an agent passes work on, it says when it will check back, and then it checks. Nothing waits on someone remembering.
