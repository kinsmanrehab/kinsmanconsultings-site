How Letter Rack works

Five steps, all on your own computer.

1. Choose a passphrase.

Install Letter Rack and choose a passphrase. Letter Rack gives you a recovery code: print it or write it down, and keep it somewhere safe. There's no reset. If you like, you can also unlock with Touch ID on a Mac or Windows Hello on a PC. Your passphrase, your recovery code and that unlock, if you turn it on, are the only ways in.

2. Add your statements.

Download your statements from your bank's and card company's websites, usually under "Statements" or "Documents". PDF and CSV both work, and so do OFX and QFX. Drag them into Letter Rack.

  • Getting ready for a separation? Add 12 to 24 months for every account.
  • Just want a budget? This month and last are enough to start.
  • Only have a statement on paper? Letter Rack can't read scans or photos yet. Download the statement from your bank's website instead, as a PDF or as a CSV, OFX or QFX export.

3. See each statement checked.

Each statement is checked against its own printed balances: opening, plus money in, minus money out, must equal the closing balance, to the cent. A month shows Checked only when every account adds up. When something doesn't, the month tells you which statement and by how much. A statement with no printed balances, as many CSV downloads have, shows Not checked: Letter Rack can't check what the bank didn't print. If a statement is missing and you can't get it, choose I can't get these. The gap stays, and your report says so plainly.

4. Sort, label and split.

Letter Rack sorts transactions into categories with a starter set of rules, and you can add your own. Anything it isn't sure about waits under Uncategorized for you to sort. Mark each account as yours, joint or theirs. Split a transaction when one payment covers two things.

5. Budget and report.

Set monthly limits by category, with rollover if you want it. See what's left until payday, by your own figures, and track savings goals. When you need a record, make a report as a PDF and CSVs. It lists the statements it's built from, each with a fingerprint, so anyone with the original files can check they weren't changed. Back up to a single encrypted file whenever you like.

Day to day

  • Lock when you step away. Letter Rack also locks itself after 5 minutes without activity.
  • Hide now puts the window away and locks it in one click.
  • Quiet names, in Privacy, keeps screen names neutral. "Separation report" becomes "Reports", for example.

What Letter Rack won't do

  • Connect to your bank, or ask for your bank login.
  • Move money or pay bills.
  • Tell you what to do with your money, or give legal advice.
  • Send your statements, or anything made from them, anywhere.
  • Reset a forgotten passphrase. Nobody can, including us.
