Three promises

On your own computer.

Your statements, and everything Letter Rack works out from them, stay on your Mac or PC, encrypted with a passphrase only you know. There's no account to create and no bank login to hand over. We don't run a server for your data, so we never see your records.

Statements in.

Drag in the PDF or CSV statements from your bank's website. OFX and QFX files work too. Letter Rack reads each one and checks that it adds up: opening balance, plus money in, minus money out, equals the closing balance, to the cent. When a month doesn't add up, or a statement is missing, it tells you which one. When it can't read a layout, it tells you that too. If a file has no printed balances, as many CSV downloads don't, Letter Rack marks it Not checked.

Budget out.

From the same statements, a budget you can use: monthly limits by category, with rollover if you want it; what's left until payday, by your own figures; savings goals; and split transactions for costs you share. Letter Rack does the arithmetic. The decisions stay yours.

For when your own numbers matter.

If you're separating.

Get a clear record of a year or two of spending, account by account, with each account marked, by you, as yours, joint or theirs. Letter Rack turns it into a report you can share with a mediator or lawyer. The report lists every source statement with a fingerprint, so anyone with the original files can check they weren't changed. It's a record of what your statements show, not legal or financial advice.

If you look after a parent's money.

Keep their statements apart from yours, check each month as it arrives, and have the figures on hand when the family asks.

If you'd rather not link your bank to an app.

Get a full budget from the statements you download yourself. There's nothing to link, and no one else's server to sync to.

Private, and plain about it.

Most money apps ask you to trust them. Letter Rack is built so there's less to take on trust, and it shows you what it can and can't reach.

Where your records live.

In one folder on your computer. Every file in it is encrypted (AES-256-GCM) and unreadable without your passphrase or recovery code. If you turn on Touch ID or Windows Hello, that computer can unlock it too.

What it can reach.

The part of Letter Rack that holds your key and reads your statements has no networking code. On a Mac, macOS blocks that part from connecting to the internet. On Windows, a Windows Firewall rule added when you install it does the same. Letter Rack's window runs in Apple's or Windows' own web component, which the Mac requires to have network permission and which the Windows rule doesn't cover. So a setting built into Letter Rack stops the window loading anything from the internet. On Windows, that component's crash reports stay on your computer. Like the rest of Windows, it sends Microsoft the diagnostic data your Windows privacy settings allow. To see this for yourself, open Privacy › What this app can reach.

What the app collects.

Nothing. Letter Rack has no account, no analytics and no tracking. If you choose to send feedback or a crash report, it goes from your own email, you see every word first, and it reaches us like any other email. This website has no analytics either. This website, and the Letter Rack downloads, are hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub logs visitors' IP addresses for security purposes, under GitHub's privacy statement. We don't receive those logs.

What's yours to look after.

There's no passphrase reset. Nobody, including us, can open your records without your passphrase, your recovery code, or the Touch ID or Windows Hello unlock you set up on that computer. Keep the recovery code somewhere safe. Backups are encrypted. PDF and CSV exports are ordinary files once you save them, and so are the statements you downloaded from your bank: Letter Rack keeps its own encrypted copy and leaves your originals where they were.

AI, only if you want it.

Letter Rack can write a summary of your categories and totals, with no names, merchants or account numbers, for you to paste into an AI tool yourself. It has no AI connector and never sends the summary anywhere.
